# Code of Conduct

[English](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) · [한국어](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.ko.md)

## Commitment

ORCA_auto welcomes people of all backgrounds and experience levels. Everyone
should be able to ask questions, contribute, and discuss research software
without harassment or discrimination, regardless of age, disability, ethnicity,
gender identity or expression, nationality, appearance, race, religion, sexual
orientation, or other personal characteristics.

## Expected behavior

- Treat people with respect and assume good intent while taking the impact of your words seriously.
- Discuss ideas, code, and evidence. Give specific, constructive feedback without attacking people.
- Welcome questions and different levels of technical knowledge, language fluency, and research experience.
- Respect personal boundaries, requests to stop, and the privacy of people and their research.
- Acknowledge mistakes, accept feedback, and make a reasonable effort to repair harm.

Respectful disagreement, reporting a concern, and constructive criticism of the
project or its maintainers are welcome.

## Unacceptable behavior

- Harassment, discrimination, slurs, or degrading comments about personal characteristics.
- Threats, intimidation, stalking, or encouraging harm against anyone.
- Unwanted sexual attention or sexualized content in project spaces.
- Personal attacks, persistent insults, or deliberately disrupting discussions.
- Publishing or threatening to publish someone's private information, private communications, or nonpublic research without permission.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith, supports a reporter, or participates in a review.

## Scope and responsibility

This policy applies to everyone, including maintainers, in project-managed
repositories, issues, pull requests, discussions, and other official community
spaces. It also applies when someone officially represents ORCA_auto in public.
Conduct outside these spaces may be considered when it directly affects safety
or participation within the project; unrelated private disagreements are outside
this policy's scope.

Maintainers are responsible for explaining these standards and applying them
consistently. Enforcement is limited to spaces and access the project controls.

## Reporting a concern

Send reports privately to **[daehyupsohn@gmail.com](mailto:daehyupsohn@gmail.com)**.
This address is monitored by the project maintainer. You may report behavior you
experienced or witnessed; you do not need to confront the person involved first.
Do not use public issues or pull requests to share sensitive allegations or
personal information.

Include only what you are comfortable sharing: what happened, when and where it
happened, relevant links or evidence, and any immediate concerns or preferred way
to follow up. Incomplete reports are welcome. Do not send passwords, access
tokens, or unrelated private research data.

Reports will be handled as privately as possible. Information will be shared
only as needed to review the concern and take action. Absolute confidentiality
cannot be guaranteed; where possible, the reviewer will discuss necessary
sharing with the reporter before proceeding. Public explanations should avoid
identifying reporters or disclosing sensitive details.

## Review and response

The maintainer will acknowledge reports when reasonably possible, review the
available information, and explain the next steps. There is no guaranteed
response time, and this address is not an emergency service. A report that cannot
be substantiated is not, by itself, evidence of a bad-faith report.

A maintainer personally involved in a report must not decide its outcome. An
independent reviewer should be sought, with the reporter's consent before
sharing the report. If none is available, that limitation must be explained to
the reporter. This project does not currently provide a separate independent
reporting address.

Responses should reflect the behavior's severity, impact, and repetition. They
may include a request to stop or correct behavior, a warning, removal of content,
or a temporary or permanent restriction on participation. Serious incidents may
warrant an immediate restriction; a warning is not a required first step.

When feasible without exposing others to further harm, the person affected by an
enforcement decision should receive an explanation and an opportunity to
respond. The reporter should receive an appropriate update, subject to the
privacy of others. Reviewers should keep a minimal private record of the reasons
for their decision and limit access to those who need it for the review.

## Requesting reconsideration

A reporter or a person subject to enforcement may request reconsideration at the
same email address, explaining any missing context, new information, or concern
about the process. A reviewer who was not involved in the original decision
should consider the request when available. Any lack of independent review must
be made clear; the project cannot promise an independent appeals panel.

## Language and updates

The English and Korean versions express the same policy and should be updated
together. Please report translation differences privately if they involve a
sensitive matter, or through a documentation issue otherwise.
